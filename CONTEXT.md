# CompetencyX

CompetencyX helps people explore plausible technology career directions and understand what evidence supports each direction.

## Language

**Role Discovery**:
A career-exploration process that ranks plausible roles, explains the supporting evidence, and communicates uncertainty. It does not determine one objectively correct career for a person.
_Avoid_: Career diagnosis, definitive role assignment

**Skill Assessment**:
The self-assessment in which the Primary Role Discovery Respondent rates their own current practice maturity across fixed competency dimensions (the PSP and SDLC tracks). It is the source of evidence for Role Readiness, as Role Discovery is the source of evidence for Role Affinity. It is a self-report of perceived capability, not a test or knowledge check.
_Avoid_: Skill test, knowledge check, competency exam, proficiency exam

**Role Evidence**:
The accumulated support for a claim about a Role, derived from the respondent's answers rather than their opinion of the role. Role Discovery answers contribute affinity evidence (how well the person's preferences fit the role); Skill Assessment answers contribute readiness evidence (how prepared their current skills are). Role Candidates are ranked by evidence, Role Confidence is the strength of that evidence, and each suggestion is explained by the evidence behind it.
_Avoid_: Score, prediction, personal opinion

**Role Candidate**:
A role with enough Role Affinity evidence to be presented as a plausible direction for further exploration.
_Avoid_: Predicted career

**Best-fit Role**:
The highest-ranked Role Candidate offered as a starting recommendation, not a declaration that the role is the person's only or objectively correct career.
_Avoid_: Correct role, assigned role

**Role Confidence**:
The strength and completeness of evidence supporting a Role Candidate relative to competing candidates. It is not the probability that the person will succeed in that career.
_Avoid_: Success probability, career certainty

**Role Resolution**:
The degree to which Role Discovery has settled on a clear Best-fit Role from the Role Evidence. It ranges across four states: in progress (still gathering answers), unknown (enough answers but no direction emerges), resolved (a clear best-fit backed by strong, well-separated evidence), and low-confidence (a best-fit exists but the evidence is weak, so it is surfaced as a tentative direction to validate in Skill Assessment). Surfacing low-confidence instead of forcing a yes/no result is how the product communicates uncertainty honestly.
_Avoid_: Accuracy, correctness, pass/fail, verdict

**Role Affinity**:
The degree to which a person's Work Activity Preferences and Domain Preferences align with a role. It is independent of the person's current skill level.
_Avoid_: Ability, readiness, qualification

**Role Readiness**:
The degree to which a person's current skills and demonstrated experience prepare them to perform a role.
_Avoid_: Role fit, interest

**Role Aspiration**:
A role direction the person wants to pursue, whether or not it currently has the highest Role Affinity or Role Readiness.
_Avoid_: Best-fit Role, predicted role

**Roadmap**:
A curated learning path for a Role, composed of Roadmap Topics in prerequisite order. It is the material a respondent works through to grow toward a role, not a measure of how ready they already are.
_Avoid_: Curriculum, course, syllabus

**Roadmap Topic**:
A single learnable subject within a Roadmap, such as a concept or skill area to study, with a difficulty and a place in the prerequisite order.
_Avoid_: Lesson, module, skill

**Assessable Topic Set**:
An authored cluster of Roadmap Topics for one Role, written so a respondent can place themselves against it in a single question. It is authored for the Role rather than read off the imported roadmap's own structure, and it names the group of topics a Held statement applies to. Its meaning is fixed by its Canonical Thai Wording; a set may be asked while that wording is still a draft, and being reviewed is a status of the set, not a precondition for it to exist. Only skills a person can practise belong in a set: industry exposure, credentials, and career logistics are not assessable and are left out even when the imported roadmap lists them.
_Avoid_: Dimension, category, topic group, node, certification, industry experience

**Recommendation Stability**:
The point at which further Skill Assessment answers stop changing which topics the product would suggest next. It is the signal to stop asking, not a measure of how correct the suggestions are.
_Avoid_: Convergence, accuracy, confidence

**Self-placed Mastery**:
The level at which the respondent themself states they can work on a Roadmap Topic, derived from their Skill Assessment rating. It is a claim the person makes about their own capability, not a measurement the product performed. The product never asserts it is correct.
_Avoid_: Mastery score, test result, proficiency level, pass/fail

**Held Topic**:
A Roadmap Topic whose Self-placed Mastery has reached the threshold at which it stops being offered as something to learn next. It stays visible on the Roadmap, marked as something the respondent said they can already do. Holding a topic is the respondent's own statement, not the product certifying the skill.
_Avoid_: Passed, completed, mastered, verified, achieved

**Unassessed Topic**:
A Roadmap Topic the respondent has neither been asked about nor marked themselves. It is distinct from a topic with low Self-placed Mastery: the product has no evidence either way, so it must not be presented as a gap or counted as absent capability.
_Avoid_: Zero mastery, missing skill, not known

**Recommendation**:
The next Roadmap Topic the product suggests a respondent learn for a specific Role, with a reason. It is produced once Role Discovery has resolved a direction, and is built for the respondent's Role Aspiration and/or their Best-fit Role. It is a starting suggestion, not a prescription or required plan.
_Avoid_: Prescription, assignment, mandatory step

**Work Activity Preference**:
A pattern of activities and problem-solving modes a person prefers, such as analysing, building, designing, testing, coordinating, operating, or communicating.
_Avoid_: Skill, technical proficiency

**Domain Preference**:
A person's relative interest in problem domains such as data, artificial intelligence, security, mobile, infrastructure, games, product, or documentation.
_Avoid_: Domain expertise, qualification

**Primary Role Discovery Respondent**:
A learner or early-career explorer who may have little or no professional technology-work experience. Role Discovery must not require prior exposure to a role, tool, or workplace practice to produce useful evidence.
_Avoid_: Experienced practitioner, job applicant

**Experience-Neutral Question**:
A question that describes recognizable work activities in plain language, so a Primary Role Discovery Respondent can express a preference without needing technical vocabulary or prior job experience. Being unable to picture an activity is distinct from having no preference between activities.
_Avoid_: Knowledge check, technology-familiarity question

**Single-Axis Question**:
A Role Discovery question that measures either Work Activity Preference or Domain Preference, never both at once. This separation keeps role evidence attributable to the person's preferred way of working or preferred problem domain.
_Avoid_: Mixed activity-domain question

**Canonical Thai Wording**:
The Thai wording that defines the meaning of an item — a Role Discovery question or an Assessable Topic Set — for the Primary Role Discovery Respondent. It is the definition, not a rendering: other languages are adaptations that must preserve the same construct and contrast rather than sources from which Thai wording is translated. It starts as a draft and becomes reviewed only when a person approves it; an agent may draft and check it but never approve it. A Role Discovery question is Experience-Neutral; an Assessable Topic Set is not — it uses the working vocabulary of the role, tool names included, because a respondent who does not recognise the term has thereby said the topic is not Held, and plainer wording would cost the respondent who already has the role in mind the anchor they need.
_Avoid_: Thai translation, machine-translated source item, title_th, simplified wording
